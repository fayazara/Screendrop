# Screendrop launch film: storylines

The one thing: **Screenshots, recordings and sharing in one native Mac app, free.**
What the viewer remembers: "it's one app that does the capture, the edit and the link."

Assumptions: 28–32 s, 1920×1080 at 30 fps, music and sound effects, no voiceover, no paid media.
Every product shot is real footage of Screendrop. No drawn UI.

## A. One capture, all the way (recommended)

Device: one object across the app. Form: object-to-object chain, no plain cuts.

A crosshair drags a marquee across the film's own opening frame and the shutter fires: the film has just been screenshotted. That capture is the thing the eye follows for 30 seconds, as a shelf card, an annotated image, a recording, a Studio timeline and finally a link.

- First two seconds: a real desktop, full frame. Crosshair enters, drags, shutter. The frame shrinks into a shelf card.
- Beats: capture the frame → card lands in the shelf → card opens into the annotator (arrow, redact, background) → the marquee turns red and becomes a recording → Studio adds zooms and captions → the timeline collapses into a share link → "Screendrop. Free for Mac." with the brew command.
- Look: the bright blue of the website hero as the ground, the real windows large on it, black heavy grotesk titles (as on the banner), yellow as the single accent.
- Sound: one upbeat track, about 110 BPM; shutter, card whoosh, pen stroke, record chime, each on its visible action.

## B. Option

Device: type carries the argument. Form: cut on the beat.

Giant keycaps fill a white frame: ⌥1, ⌥2, ⌥3, ⌥4, ⌥5. Each press is a drum hit and a hard cut into the real thing that key does, full bleed, for one bar.

- First two seconds: a white frame, a keycap ⌥ the height of the screen, pressed. Cut to a real fullscreen capture landing.
- Beats: ⌥1 fullscreen → ⌥2 window → ⌥3 area → ⌥5 text off the screen onto the clipboard → ⌥4 record → Studio → link → end card typed as a key combo.
- Look: white ground, black ink, keycaps with real depth, blue accent on the pressed key only.
- Sound: percussive, 120 BPM, a key click on every cut.

## C. The film that edits itself (the bold one)

Device: the result first. Form: one frame that rebuilds.

The film opens as a flat, raw screen recording. The camera pulls back: it is playing inside Screendrop Studio. Each Studio control then changes the film you are watching: add a zoom and the film zooms, switch on captions and the film is captioned, pick a wallpaper and the frame gains its background.

- First two seconds: a plain, slightly dull recording of a desktop, full frame, with a timecode running. Pull back to reveal the Studio window around it.
- Beats: raw recording → reveal Studio → zoom block added, film zooms → cursor smoothing → captions on → background and camera layout → export → the exported file becomes a link → end card.
- Look: the real Studio window is the world for the whole film; titles sit in the film's own caption style.
- Sound: starts dry (room tone, raw clicks), music arrives when the first edit lands.
